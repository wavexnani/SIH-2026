CORE METHOD

Uncertainty changes the corridor — not merely the cost function

Dynamic obstacles are converted into time-indexed forbidden regions. Prediction uncertainty expands those regions before MPC sees the corridor.
03 / 06

Dynamic uncertainty-expanded corridor

δyunc(τ) = 2 σv τ
ylower = ŷ + v̂yτ − rag − δyunc
yupper = ŷ + v̂yτ + rag + δyunc

Why this matters

  • Prediction error grows with look-ahead time.
  • The usable free-space corridor therefore contracts as uncertainty grows.
  • The planner can reject geometrically possible but uncertainty-sensitive paths.
  • At a non-traversable bottleneck, the safe action is controlled standstill rather than forced passage.
Interpretation: 2σ corresponds to approximately 95.45% Gaussian coverage under the assumed model. This is an engineering uncertainty margin, not a formal probabilistic guarantee.

Curvature-aware velocity planning

Circular-arc validation: R ≈ 18.5 m gives κ ≈ 0.054054 m⁻¹. Smooth quintic chicane: peak |κ| ≈ 0.05344 m⁻¹. Reference lateral acceleration at 6.84 m/s ≈ 2.50 m/s² versus ≈5.34 m/s² at 10 m/s for the same reference curvature.

Failure is informative

  • Level 3: 40 collisions occurred under +0.02 rad steering bias.
  • Level 6: 100 boundary violations came from an over-tight road/pass corridor configuration; no collisions.
  • Config C chicane: 0.50 rad/s steering slew limitation caused phase lag during rapid reversal.
  • These failures identify concrete next engineering changes rather than being hidden from the benchmark.
SCENARIOS · ROBUSTNESS · ROADMAP

Designed around Indian-road failure modes, with explicit validation boundaries

The architecture is scenario-oriented: every difficult traffic condition maps to a specific planning, prediction, control, or safety mechanism.
05 / 06

01 · Unmarked village road

FreeSpaceMap generates lateral bounds without relying on lane markings.

02 · Unsignalized intersection

Interaction classification + predicted agent motion inform yield/conflict handling.

03 · Highway merge

Dynamic corridor and speed planning handle slower traffic without fixed-lane assumptions.

04 · Dense market area

Topology changes and narrow gaps are handled through free-space feasibility.

05 · Sudden cattle crossing

Prediction + coordination triggers controlled YIELD and safe deceleration.

06 · Impassable bottleneck

When a gap is not physically traversable, the safety policy commands standstill rather than forcing a maneuver.

RESEARCH BASIS · CONTRIBUTION · EVIDENCE MAP

Engineering contribution and traceable evidence

The proposed system combines established control methods with a lane-independent, uncertainty-aware planning architecture targeted at unstructured Indian traffic.

Technical contribution

Observed environment → dynamic free-space corridor
Prediction + uncertainty → time-indexed safe topology
Topology / behavior → feasible trajectory
Curvature → velocity demand
Trajectory → constrained LTV/QP MPC
Command → independent OBB/SAT safety veto

System-level proposition

Instead of forcing unstructured traffic into a lane-centric abstraction, the planner treats the road as a dynamic feasible-space problem. Prediction, uncertainty, behavior selection, geometry, and control are therefore connected through explicit interfaces.
